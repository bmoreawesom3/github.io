CS-330: Computational Graphics & Visualization

A 3D scene recreated in C++ and OpenGL — modeling a set of everyday objects with textures and lighting, and a fully navigable free-look camera that switches between perspective and orthographic views.

The scene recreates four objects from everyday life — a pyramid, a cube, a cylinder, and the kitchen table they sat on (a plane). They came from my daughter's toys, already on the table, which made them a natural fit: simple enough to model cleanly while still exercising the full pipeline of vertices, colors, and texture coordinates.

How it was built

The pyramid, cube, and plane are drawn with GL_TRIANGLES, while the cylinder uses GL_TRIANGLE_FAN and GL_TRIANGLE_STRIP. Each object defines its own vertices, indices, color formatting, and texture-coordinate data. Navigation is handled through seven key functions plus mouse input: six standard camera movements and a seventh that switches between perspective and orthographic projection.

Camera controls

  • W / A / S / D — move forward, left, back, and right.
  • Q / E — move up and down along the Y axis.
  • P — hold to switch from perspective to an orthographic view.
  • Mouse — free-look camera; scroll wheel zooms in and out.

Reflection

I approached each function as a main task with smaller subtasks beneath it, which made the program easier to navigate — I could collapse or expand sections instead of scrolling through the whole file. I kept the objects together in one place rather than splitting them across classes, because that made the code easier for me to follow, and I leaned on thorough comments and color-coded highlighting to keep the sections readable. One idea I'd build on next is a height-adjustment setting for the camera, so a viewer could walk through the scene from their own eye level rather than the fixed elevated default.
